# The Elegance of Minimalism — Extraction Notes

## PDF Metadata
- **Title:** Web UI Trends Present & Future: The Elegance of Minimalism
- **Authors:** Jerry Cao & Carrie Cousins, UXPin Inc., 2015
- **Page count:** 36 pages

## Core Theses
1. Minimalism is **timeless, not trendy** — roots 100+ years old (Bernhard, early 1900s).
2. Sculpture through subtraction — "perfection is when nothing more can be taken away."
3. Minimalism is intentional, not barren. Philosophy that prioritizes content.
4. Only works with **rigorous underlying structure** (grid, balance, alignment, contrast).
5. **Not universal** — fails for ad-heavy, feature-heavy, e-commerce, younger audiences.
6. Evolving toward "almost flat" — minimal texture, motion, asymmetry.
7. **Beware too-minimal** — pure icon nav, linear-only flows, hidden affordances harm usability.

## Principles

### 1. Less But Better (Subtractive Mindset) (p. 7, 24)
Iterate by removing. At each step remove as much as possible.

### 2. Content-First Philosophy (p. 8, 23, 33)
**Philosophy that prioritizes content, not a visual style.**

### 3. Necessary Elements Only (p. 24)
Logo, navigation, body content, contact. Discard the rest.

### 4. One Idea Per Page/Scroll Section (p. 24)
Reduces cognitive load. Creates rhythm.

### 5. Five-or-Fewer Major Sections (p. 24) — HARD RULE
Hick's Law. Cap primary nav at 5.

### 6. Hick's Law — One CTA Per Page (p. 24)
One destination/action besides basic nav.

### 7. Design B&W First (p. 24)
Validate hierarchy and structure independent of color. Add color after.

### 8. Restrained Palette: 1 Color + Neutrals (p. 8) — HARD RULE
Single accent reads as intentional.

### 9. Negative Space ≠ White Only (p. 13, 30)
Can be white, black, very dark, or full color. Texture excluded.

### 10. Generous Negative Space = Luxury (p. 14)
Spacing scale: 8/16/24/40/64/96. Large vertical rhythm.

### 11. Strong Grid Is Backbone (p. 15)
12-col grid with strict baseline rhythm. Every element snaps.

### 12. Alignment ≠ Centering (p. 15)
Text can align left/right/center along grid. Default body = left.

### 13. Four Forms of Balance (p. 16-18)
Horizontal symmetry, approximate symmetry, radial symmetry, asymmetry. Pick deliberately.

### 14. Contrast as Hierarchy Mechanism (p. 19-20)
When decoration is gone, contrast is the only hierarchy tool. Type-size contrast > color contrast.

### 15. Avoid Microcopy-Sized Body (p. 20)
Display contrast must not break readability. Min body 16-18px.

### 16. Stellar Typography as Focal Point (p. 12, 20)
Distinctive display + neutral body. Type carries identity when ornament is removed.

### 17. Hamburger Menu Has Costs (p. 11-12)
Reduces discoverability. Less clear over 44 years old. Keep desktop nav visible.

### 18. Don't Hide Behind Pure Icon Nav (p. 32)
Confuses when metaphors aren't straightforward. Icons + text labels in primary nav.

### 19. Don't Force Linear-Only Exploration (p. 32)
Provide breadcrumbs, search, tag pages, jump-to anchors.

### 20. Re-embed Functional Affordances (p. 32)
Pendulum swinging back. Visible "Read more," scroll indicators, link affordances.

### 21. Spirit Over Literal Aesthetic (p. 23, 33)
Don't mistake expression for intent. Document why each constraint exists.

### 22. Density Increases Down the Scroll (p. 22)
High-level at top (sparse), denser deeper. Article opens airy, body gets denser.

### 23. Z-Shaped Reading & Alternating Layout (p. 23)
Long pages need rhythm changes. Alternate code-on-right with prose-on-left.

### 24. Flat Design Pairs with Minimalism (p. 9-10)
Flat icons, solid-fill buttons, no skeuomorphic shadows.

### 25. "Almost Flat" Evolution (p. 29-30)
Subtle 1px borders, soft shadows, pale background textures permitted.

### 26. Reverse Colors / Monotone Schemes (p. 30)
Dark backgrounds with lighter text. Tonal scale of one hue. **Legitimizes dark mode within minimalism.**

### 27. Motion Is Allowed (p. 31)
"Minimal does not equal static." 150-250ms fades, smooth scroll, hover underlines.

### 28. Reveal-on-Hover for Density (p. 26)
Progressive disclosure preserves sparseness.

### 29. When Minimalism Fails (p. 25-27)
Ad-heavy, feature-heavy, complex e-commerce, children/young audiences. Tech blog/portfolio = good fit.

### 30. Performance Benefit (p. 28)
Less information = faster load. Performance budgets per page (<100kB CSS).

### 31. Responsive Compatibility (p. 28)
Sparse layouts collapse cleanly to mobile.

### 32. Drop Common Clutter (p. 24)
Omit: social media icons, bulky footers/sitemaps, "recent posts" widgets.

### 33. Color-Field Backgrounds as Negative Space (p. 13-14)
Bright colored backgrounds can act as negative space (Lindvall aqua).

### 34. Know Your Users First (p. 27)
"Don't apply a trend just because it's hip. Know your users, create personas, then make sure the decision helps them."

### 35. Never Delete Irreplaceable Functionality (p. 33) — SHARPEST WARNING
"Never misinterpret minimalism as a design goal, otherwise you'll just as likely find yourself deleting elements with irreplaceable functionalities."

## Key Quotes
1. "Perfection is achieved not when there's nothing more to add, but when there's nothing more you can take away." (p. 7)
2. "Minimalism works because it does what all design should do — put the focus and emphasis on content." (p. 8)
3. "If flat design is a trendy new ingredient... minimalism is the classic cookbook." (p. 10)
4. "Generous negative space creates a sense of luxury." (p. 14)
5. "Minimal does not equal static." (p. 31)
6. "Never misinterpret minimalism as a design goal." (p. 33)
